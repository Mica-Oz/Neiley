Colorado Divorce Timeline

Colorado law requires at least 91 days between the date the court gains jurisdiction over the other spouse (usually when they are served, or when they respond) and the earliest date a decree can be entered.

Enter a date to see the earliest possible decree date.

An estimate for general information only, not legal advice. Most cases take longer, depending on negotiation, the court’s calendar, and whether issues are contested.

  1. Initial Consultation

    We meet with you to understand your situation, explain your rights and options, discuss likely outcomes, and outline our approach to your case.

  2. Filing and Service

    One spouse files a Petition for Dissolution of Marriage with the court. The other spouse is served with the petition and has an opportunity to respond.

  3. Temporary Orders

    If immediate issues need to be addressed, such as who stays in the family home, temporary parenting time, or temporary support, we can request temporary orders.

  4. Discovery

    Both parties exchange financial information and documents, including financial affidavits, tax returns, bank and retirement statements, and business records.

  5. Negotiation

    We work to negotiate a settlement addressing all issues, through direct negotiation, mediation, or collaborative processes.

  6. Trial, if Needed

    If settlement cannot be reached, the case proceeds to trial, where a judge decides the contested issues.

  7. Decree

    Once all issues are resolved, by agreement or court decision, the court enters a Decree of Dissolution of Marriage finalizing your divorce.
